How the prediction is made

Three stages run continuously, so the map above is never more than one satellite pass old.

01

Historical record

The last ~15 months of real satellite fire detections, cross-referenced against the weather recorded at the same place and time, form the training set.

02

Live inputs

Each satellite pass brings fresh thermal readings, vegetation dryness and local weather into the model.

03

Risk on the map

The model scores every monitored area and publishes the result straight to the public map, hourly.

Recent fire history

Every fire the system has detected and tracked this season. These records feed straight back into the model that produces the risk map above.

Records shown are real NASA FIRMS satellite detections from the last 3 weeks, clustered by location. The model above is trained on the last ~15 months of this same detection archive.

Reading the score

Every monitored area gets a 0-100 ignition risk score for the day. What each band means:

0-39 · Low to moderate

Conditions do not favour ignition. Routine monitoring; no change to normal posture.

40-59 · High

Fuels are dry enough to carry fire. Worth watching wind forecasts and access routes.

60-79 · Very high

An ignition here is likely to spread. Crews and aircraft are usually pre-positioned.

80-100 · Extreme

Rapid spread expected on ignition. Highest priority for detection and early response.
